# Known limitations

- **Foreground only.** Proactive check-ins happen while Questling is open. Even’s FAQ: no push notifications, WebView suspends in the background. Stale reminders are discarded on resume.
- **Calendar linking needs a second browser.** Google forbids OAuth in embedded WebViews and the SDK cannot open the system browser, so users open a short link on their phone/computer and enter a code. The link must reach the Questling server.
- **Calendar sessions are short.** ~1 hour (`access_type=online`, no refresh token, memory-only). Reconnect afterwards. A server restart also ends sessions.
- **Voice requires the server and a voice-capable provider (Gemini, or OpenAI transcription).** Claude, OpenRouter and Ollama are text-only here; their voice requests are answered by Gemini/OpenAI and labelled as such. No on-device speech recognition; offline voice is shown as unavailable rather than faked.
- **Extra AI providers vary.** Quality, latency, refusals and JSON reliability differ by model; every reply is validated, and invalid output falls back to a labelled local reply. Small Ollama models may often fail validation. Gemini is the default; the others can be left unconfigured.
- **Task lists are import + tick-off only.** Questling reads open tasks (title, due date) and can mark one done after confirmation. It does not create, edit, move or delete tasks, and does not sync continuously — press *Refresh*. Google Tasks uses the default list only; Notion uses one shared database whose shape it can recognise (title + Done checkbox or Status with a “Complete” group). Sessions last at most 12 h and end on server restart; reconnect afterwards. Todoist recurring tasks complete only the current occurrence.
- **Mail & chat are heads-ups, not a mail client.** Gmail: unread inbox mail only, sender + subject (metadata scope — Questling cannot read bodies, search or show attachments); a heads-up fires only for Gmail’s *Important* label; replies go to the original sender only (no reply-all, no new emails). `gmail.metadata` is a Google *restricted* scope: fine for test users of an app in Testing, but a public release needs Google verification and a security assessment. Slack: only messages from the last 3 days that @mention you (no DMs, no channel reading); replies go into that thread. `search.messages` is a Slack legacy method — it works for user tokens today but Slack recommends the newer Real-time Search API, which would need an app-directory or internal app. Sessions last ≤ 12 h and end on server restart. Inboxes refresh while the app is open only.
- **Ask my notes reads only what you share.** Questling sees the Notion pages you shared with its connection. Notion's search matches page titles, so Questling searches the question's keywords plus your most recently edited pages, reads up to five candidates (top-level text blocks, not sub-pages, tables or files) and answers from at most three. Without an AI provider the answer is the best matching lines, labelled.
- **The channel digest needs your bot in the chat.** Telegram only delivers channel posts to bots that are channel admins, and full group chatter only when the bot's privacy mode is off (or it is a group admin). Posts are kept in server memory for up to 2 days, 30 per chat, 20 chats.
- **Telegram is bot-based.** Questling can only see what Telegram lets your bot see: messages to the bot, group messages that mention or reply to it (BotFather privacy mode), and — with a Business connection — the private chats you selected. Business replies are possible only within 24 h of the incoming message and with *Reply to messages* allowed. Use a dedicated bot: one with a webhook, or polled by another program, can't be shared (`telegram_conflict`). The message buffer lives in server memory and is lost on restart.
- **AI features need a key.** Without an AI provider, catch-up, prep, recap and triage use local, labelled summaries built from your data; AI reply suggestions, summaries and translations are hidden. With a key they cost one request each (triage at most every 10 minutes when the inbox changes).
- **GitHub needs a classic token** (GitHub's notifications API rejects fine-grained tokens). Only review requests, mentions, assignments and approval requests are shown; it never comments or merges.
- **Training's glasses step** is completed by an actual tap on the glasses (the demo simulates it from the phone and says so).
- **Focus sessions live in memory**: a restart ends the session (the quest stays in progress).
- **Plan my day is deterministic.** It orders quests by status and deadline and fits them into free calendar gaps in your active hours (late in the evening it plans tomorrow). It doesn't estimate effort or energy; ask the companion to refine it when an AI provider is configured.
- **Full 18+ may not pass store review.** Even Hub content rules may reject crude adult humour even when opt-in and adult-attested. A build flag to ship without 18+ mode is not implemented.
- **Primary calendar only**; no attendee invitations; no deletes (by design).
- **Profanity detection is heuristic** (word patterns) — defence in depth on top of the AI provider’s own safety settings, not a classifier.
- **Not built yet:** JSON backup export/import and a glasses boss screen. Boss campaigns, items, achievements and task-list import exist on the phone.
- **Single-process server state** (rate limits, budget, link codes, Calendar/task/mail sessions) is lost on restart and not shared across instances.
- **Package size** `.ehpk` ≈ 3.0 MB including fonts, painted scenes (WebP) and sprites, under the ~10 MB practical cap. Scenes use WebP (supported by the Android WebView and iOS 14+ WebKit).
- **Companion has no tab**: it is reached from the Today scene, the floating portrait on other tabs and notices. The floating portrait can cover a few pixels of content at the bottom-right; it steps aside while scrolling and is not shown on Camp, which has a *Your companion* row instead.
